Text-to-CAD Just Got Real: The CADDY Model Breakdown

This guide explores the CADDY model architecture (released May 2026), which revolutionizes Text-to-CAD by shifting from pixel-based generation to graph-based representations. It details how constrained decoding ensures the generation of valid, manufacturable STEP files directly from text prompts, effectively solving the 'hallucination geometry' problem inherent in previous AI models.

📝 Step-by-Step Guide

1

Step 1: Adopt Graph-Based Representation

Instead of predicting raw coordinates in 3D space, frame the CAD generation task as a sequence of tokens. Treat the build history as a connected graph where operations (nodes) are linked logically.

2

Step 2: Implement Constrained Decoding

Apply a 'grammar' to the generation process. Ensure that dependencies are strictly followed, such as ensuring an extrusion operation only follows a valid sketch defined on a plane.

3

Step 3: Validate Structural Integrity

Configure the decoder to reject invalid geometric proposals in real-time. If a generation step would create a hole that breaks the object's manifoldness or structural integrity, the decoder blocks it.

4

Step 4: Generate Manufacturable STEP Files

Export the final graph-based output as a standard STEP file. This ensures the recipient receives not just a visual mesh, but a solid body with a valid construction tree.

💡 Pro Tips

Pro Tip: Focus on the construction tree logic rather than just visual similarity when evaluating generated parts.
Pro Tip: Use the Fusion360 Gallery benchmark to test your own constraint satisfaction algorithms.
Pro Tip: When prompting, be specific about operational sequences (e.g., 'sketch on plane, then extrude') to align with the model's token-based logic.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Watch Out: Pitfall 1: Floating point drift: Attempting to predict absolute 3D coordinates leads to misaligned faces and non-manifold geometry.
Watch Out: Pitfall 2: Ignoring the construction tree: Generating a mesh without the underlying operational history results in a 'visual' part that cannot be easily edited or manufactured.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

What is the main problem CADDY solves?

CADDY solves the 'hallucination geometry' problem where previous models generated parts that looked correct but had impossible dimensions or non-manifold geometry.

How does CADDY ensure parts are manufacturable?

It uses constrained decoding to enforce strict rules (like a grammar) during generation, ensuring that features like holes and extrusions maintain structural integrity and result in valid STEP files.

What is a graph-based representation in this context?

It treats the CAD design as a sequence of connected operations (tokens) rather than a blob of pixels, ensuring that the history of how the part was built is preserved and logical.
