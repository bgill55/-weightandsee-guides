🎯 Key Takeaways

  • 1Gemma-4-12B-IT is optimized for efficiency, consuming significantly less power and VRAM than Llama-4.
  • 2Llama-4 prioritizes raw throughput, making it a better choice for high-end hardware like the RTX 4090.
  • 3Both models are capable of completing complex refactoring tasks in under one second.
  • 4Gemma-4's 'lean' architecture allows for better multitasking on mid-range hardware like RTX 4070s or Apple M3 chips.

  • 6Hardware selection should be based on available VRAM headroom rather than just raw speed, as both models are highly performant.

📋 Prerequisites

  • Local environment with a GPU (NVIDIA RTX 4070+ or Apple Silicon M3 recommended).
  • Basic familiarity with running local LLMs via Ollama, LM Studio, or HuggingFace transformers.

💡 Pro Tips

Pro Tip: Use 4-bit quantization to fit larger models into mid-range VRAM without significant degradation in coding logic.
Pro Tip: Monitor GPU utilization using 'nvidia-smi' or 'asitop' (for Mac) while running refactors to identify thermal throttling.
Pro Tip: If you have limited VRAM, prioritize Gemma-4 to keep your IDE and browser responsive while the model runs.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Watch Out: VRAM Over-allocation: Attempting to run Llama-4 on lower-end cards without quantization will lead to OOM (Out of Memory) errors.
Watch Out: Ignoring Background Processes: Forgetting that your IDE and browser consume VRAM can cause stuttering during model inference.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

Which model is better for a MacBook M3?

Gemma-4-12B-IT is generally better for Apple Silicon due to its lower VRAM/memory footprint, allowing for smoother multitasking.

Does Llama-4 always perform better at coding?

Not necessarily; while Llama-4 offers higher throughput, Gemma-4-12B-IT is often just as accurate for standard refactoring tasks while being significantly more power-efficient.

Can I run these models without a dedicated GPU?

Yes, but performance will be significantly slower. Using a CPU-only setup will likely result in multi-second delays, negating the benefits of 'real-time' refactoring.
