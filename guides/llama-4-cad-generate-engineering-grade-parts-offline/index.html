Step 2: Pull the Llama 4 CAD Weights

Download the specialized Llama 4 CAD weights in GGUF format. These weights are optimized for generating mathematical instructions for geometry rather than just descriptive text.

ollama run llama-4-70b-cad
3

Step 3: Prompting for Parametric Parts

Input specific engineering constraints into the model. Instead of visual descriptions, use technical parameters such as hole diameters, load-bearing requirements, and specific dimensions.

Prompt: 'Generate a 6061 aluminum mounting bracket, 5mm wall thickness, 4x M6 clearance holes, 50mm center-to-center spacing, output in STEP format.'
4

Step 4: Geometry Verification

Export the generated code into a CAD viewer or verification tool to check for manifold errors and ensure the B-Rep logic is sound before sending it to manufacturing.

💡 Pro Tips

Pro Tip: Specify exact material properties in your prompt to help the model better calculate necessary wall thicknesses.
Pro Tip: Use high-quantization GGUF files (Q8_0 or higher) to minimize geometric errors caused by weight compression.
Pro Tip: Always perform a 'manifold check' in software like FreeCAD or Rhino before committing to a CNC toolpath.

⚠️ Common Pitfalls

Watch Out: VRAM Bottlenecks: Attempting to run the 70B model on consumer cards with less than 24GB of VRAM will lead to extreme latency or crashes.
Watch Out: Mesh Confusion: Do not confuse these outputs with STLs; these are parametric instructions. Converting them back to low-quality meshes loses the engineering precision.
Watch Out: Over-reliance on Defaults: Always explicitly state tolerances (e.g., +/- 0.1mm) or the model may default to generic 'visual' approximations.

❓ Frequently Asked Questions

How does this differ from generating 3D models with Midjourney or DALL-E?

Visual AI generates 'dumb' meshes (triangles) which are often physically impossible. Llama 4 CAD generates 'smart' parametric data (STEP/B-Rep) that follows engineering rules and can be edited in professional CAD software.

Can I use these files for CNC machining?

Yes. Because the model outputs B-Rep and STEP data, the geometry is precise enough for CAM software to generate toolpaths for CNC mills and lathes.

Is my design data sent to Meta or any cloud provider?

No. By using the GGUF weights locally via Ollama, all inference happens on your local GPU, ensuring your intellectual property never leaves your machine.
